## Methodology

Rules were sourced from official government and intergovernmental websites only. Each country was cross-checked against at least one primary source. Where regulations changed in 2025–2026 (EU Pet Passport mandate, Singapore CAPQ restructure, CDC dog import form) the latest effective rule is recorded. All data is accurate as of May 2026 and should be re-verified annually or after any policy update.

## Country-by-Country Notes

### Australia (AU) — HIGH COMPLEXITY
Australia has some of the strictest biosecurity rules in the world. The critical path for most relocating employees is:
1. Microchip the pet (ISO 15-digit).
2. Administer rabies vaccination.
3. Wait 30+ days, then draw blood for the RNATT titre test at an approved laboratory.
4. **Wait 180 days** from the blood draw date before travel is permitted.
5. Apply for an import permit from DAFF (Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry) at least 5 weeks before travel.
6. All pets undergo a mandatory 10-night quarantine at the Mickleham facility near Melbourne.

Total minimum lead time: **~8–9 months** from first vet appointment to arrival.

### New Zealand (NZ) — HIGH COMPLEXITY
Similar to Australia. Australia-origin pets skip the RNATT requirement but still undergo 10-day quarantine. Import permit must be in hand before booking a flight. Only Air New Zealand and Qantas carry pets to NZ.

### United Kingdom (GB) — MEDIUM COMPLEXITY
Post-Brexit, EU Pet Passports are no longer valid for entry into Great Britain. A new Animal Health Certificate (AHC) must be obtained from an Official Veterinarian within 10 days of each trip. Dogs uniquely require tapeworm (Echinococcus) treatment 24–120 hours before arrival — this step is frequently overlooked by relocation managers.

### Japan (JP) — HIGH COMPLEXITY
Japan's import protocol is sequential and non-negotiable:
1. Microchip (ISO 15-digit).
2. Two primary rabies vaccinations using **inactivated vaccines only** (modified-live vaccines are rejected).
3. Blood draw for RNATT titre test.
4. 180-day wait from the titre test blood draw.
5. Submit notification to the Animal Quarantine Service (AQS) **40 days before arrival**.
6. Health certificate within 10 days of arrival.

Failure to complete any step in sequence resets the process. Non-compliant pets can be quarantined for up to 180 days at the owner's expense (~¥30,000–50,000/day). This is the single most important country to flag early in any Japan relocation involving pets.

### Singapore (SG) — MEDIUM COMPLEXITY
Singapore uses a schedule-based system: pets from approved low-risk countries (Schedule I) require no quarantine; those from high-risk countries (Schedule III) face 30 days. **From April 2026**, all pet imports must be processed exclusively through AVS-recognised agents at the Central Animal Processing Quarantine (CAPQ) facility — direct owner imports are no longer permitted. Breed restrictions apply for dogs (Pit Bull types, Akitas banned).

### EU Countries (FR, DE, NL, ES, IT, PT) — LOW COMPLEXITY (for compliant pets)
All six countries follow EU Regulation 576/2013 uniformly. The key 2026 change is that **EU Pet Passports became mandatory for intra-EU travel from 22 April 2026**. For non-EU residents relocating to the EU, an Animal Health Certificate (AHC) is required per trip (valid 10 days from issue, 4 months for return trips within the EU).

Country-specific notes:
- **DE**: Breed bans under HundVerbrEinfG cover Pit Bulls, American Staffordshires, Staffordshire Bulls, Bull Terriers at federal level; individual Bundesland rules may add more.
- **ES**: PPP breed list (RD 287/2002) requires liability insurance and muzzles for dangerous breeds.
- **PT**: Dangerous breed list under Decreto-Lei 315/2009; affected breeds cannot be rehomed.
- **IT**: All resident dogs must be registered in the national anagrafe canina within 30 days of arrival.

### Switzerland (CH) — LOW COMPLEXITY
Switzerland is not an EU member but has adopted equivalent pet movement legislation via bilateral agreements. EU AHCs and EU Pet Passports are accepted. Dogs must be registered in the AMICUS national database within 10 days of establishing residence.

### United Arab Emirates (AE) — MEDIUM COMPLEXITY
MOCCAE controls all pet imports. An import permit (valid 90 days) is mandatory and must be obtained before the pet travels. A hard limit of 2 pets per person per year is enforced at the border. Core vaccinations (DHPP/DHPL for dogs; FVRCP for cats) are required in addition to rabies.

### Canada (CA) — LOW COMPLEXITY
Canada applies minimal federal requirements. Dogs over 3 months old need a valid rabies certificate; dogs under 8 months from commercial sources need a health cert. Cats have no federal requirements. Provincial rules apply post-entry (e.g., Ontario mandates rabies vaccination within 30 days of residency).

### United States (US) — LOW-MEDIUM COMPLEXITY
The most significant recent change: **all dogs entering the US must complete the CDC Dog Import Form online before arrival**, regardless of country of origin (rule effective 2024). Microchip and rabies vaccination requirements apply only to dogs from high-risk countries as defined by the CDC. Cats have no federal requirements.

## Data Quality and Limitations

- Rules are verified at the national/federal level. Subnational (state, province, emirate) restrictions are noted in the `notes` field where material but are not exhaustive.
- Quarantine durations for Singapore are stored as `0` (the best-case Schedule I/II outcome); the `notes` field documents the Schedule III 30-day risk.
- Japan's `quarantine_days = 0` similarly reflects the best-case outcome; the notes explain the conditional quarantine risk.
- Airline-specific requirements (health certificate formats, carrier acceptance policies, breed restrictions for cabin/cargo) are **not** captured in this table and should be communicated separately.
- Rules change frequently. `last_verified_at` is set to `2026-05-01`. A scheduled re-verification at 6-month intervals is recommended.
