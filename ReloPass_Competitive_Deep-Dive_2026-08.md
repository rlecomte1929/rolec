# ReloPass — Competitive Landscape Deep-Dive

*Prepared 2026-08-12 · Source: ReloPass_Competitors.xlsx (158 companies, compiled 2026-08-11). Funding, ownership and AI claims move fast — re-verify any single fact before external citation.*

---

## 1. The landscape at a glance

158 companies, five tiers. The important move is not counting competitors — it's separating who you **fight**, who you **ride**, and who could **swallow** you.

| Tier | # | What it is | ReloPass relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Direct** | 34 | Pure-play mobility/relocation SaaS sold to corporate HR | **Compete** — same buyer, same job |
| **T2 — RMC + platform** | 50 | Relocation management companies with proprietary tech | **Compete / displace** — same budget line, services-led |
| **T3 — Immigration software** | 36 | Own the immigration slice of a case | **Overlap, not substitution** — partner or out-execute on depth |
| **T4 — Mobility tax & data** | 9 | Big Four + data houses (Mercer, AIRINC, EY, KPMG…) | **Mostly partner** — win on trust/data, lose on speed/price |
| **T5 — Adjacent** | 29 | EOR, HCM, housing, expense platforms | **Absorption threat + supply layer** — ride most, fear a few |

The single most important line in this whole analysis: **breadth is table stakes and "AI-native" is a slogan everyone uses.** Topia, CapRelo, xpath.global, Centuro ("AI Decision Engine"), Weichert ("AI Move Summary"), Jobbatical, Permitree, Pebl ("Alfie"), WorkMotion ("Wanda"), Safeguard ("Intelligent Workforce") — the entire field claims AI and multi-country coverage. Neither differentiates you. What almost nobody has is the two things your North Star names: **the SME HR generalist as the buyer, and provable, maintained correctness of the non-obvious.**

## 2. Tier 1 — Direct competitors (34): what they have that ReloPass doesn't

These are the closest analogues. The honest capability gaps:

- **Scale and proof.** Localyze (YC S2019, Germany) — 10,000+ relocations, 4.9 on G2; "the single most similar European venture-backed competitor." Jobbatical — 17,000+ relocations, AI case summaries and automated visa assessments, Workday/BambooHR integrations. ReloPass has neither the volume nor the logo wall yet.
- **Mature integrations.** Jobbatical, Settly (Mews, Bynder, Optiver, Personio), premote (SAP/Personio/Workday/HiBob/Concur/Navan/TravelPerk/Slack — the best integration story in the segment). Integrations are how this category actually gets adopted; ReloPass's are thin.
- **AI feasibility engines already shipped.** **Permitree** (Switzerland) is the closest existing AI-native feasibility engine — "LinkedIn profile + job post → eligibility, timeline, cost, risk." Centuro Global won the FEM EMEA EMMA 2025 for its AI Decision Engine. This is *exactly* the "surface the unknowns" instinct — already in market, in 11–170 countries.
- **The "coordination layer" positioning is taken.** **Workia** (US, backed by Equus) is a "lightweight mobility workspace" — the closest positioning to ReloPass. **Ineo/MoveTrack** literally markets itself as "the system of record for global mobility" — your exact category claim. **Topia** claims "AI-native, not bolted on" — your exact differentiation claim.

**Read:** You are not first, not biggest, and not the only "AI-native coordination layer." The direct tier proves the *category* is real and crowded — which is why the wedge has to be the buyer and the proof, not the pitch.

## 3. Tier 2 — RMC + platform (50): the incumbents you displace

Legacy relocation management companies that bolted tech onto a services business: SIRVA/BGRS (190+ countries, absorbed BGRS and Allied — "the status quo ReloPass displaces"), Cartus (185+, owns MovePro360), Graebel, Santa Fe, Sterling Lexicon, Plus Relocation, CapRelo ("most aggressive AI-native positioning of any legacy RMC — closest messaging competitor").

**What they have that ReloPass doesn't:** deep vendor/DSP networks and the muscle to physically execute a move (household goods, destination services, on-the-ground settling-in), decades of enterprise trust, and true global coverage. Aires productised its tech as **MobilityX** (2025) and Anywr is rolling up the European market (Map, Link Mobilité, Nordic Relocation Group, Cooptalis) — the tier is actively consolidating and tech-enabling.

**Their weakness (your opening):** services-margin businesses with legacy UX, slow to change, and structurally built for enterprise mobility *managers* — not the SME generalist. They are replaceable by an AI-native product on the *knowledge and coordination* layer, but you should **not** try to out-execute them on the physical move. That's a low-margin trap your North Star already flags.

## 4. Tier 3 — Immigration software (36): overlap, not substitution

Own the immigration slice. Two camps:

- **Enterprise / law-firm incumbents:** Fragomen (48 offices, 170+ countries, Fragomen Technologies, plus Nomadic for business travel), BAL (Cobalt), Envoy Global ("every new entrant publishes 'Envoy alternatives' pages"), Newland Chase/CIBT, Mitratech (INSZoom/Tracker roll-up).
- **Well-funded AI-native insurgents — the ones to watch:** **Manifest OS raised $60M (April 2026)**; **LegalOS is YC W26**; Casium ($5M, Oct 2025, 90 countries); Alma ("AI-native business immigration… aggressive SEO targeting Envoy/Fragomen/BAL"); Gale Visa (YC); Parley (YC, AI petition drafting). Plus document-ingestion analogues worth benchmarking — **CampLegal auto-parses USCIS notices**, Visalaw.ai is "the best example of domain-grounded RAG in this vertical."

**What they have that ReloPass doesn't:** deep filing automation, attorney networks, and petition-drafting depth. **What they don't do:** the *whole* relocation (housing, tax, banking, schools, settling-in), and they skew US-centric and firm-side or enterprise. Formally, "helping businesses bring immigration work in-house" (Formally, NZ) is *directly your thesis* — but for a different buyer.

**Read:** Immigration is a slice, not the job. Partner or integrate for filing execution (VisaHQ is API-first and "a potential partner as much as competitor"); differentiate by being the coordination + accuracy layer across the *entire* move for the SME generalist, not another visa tool.

## 5. Tier 4 — Mobility tax & data (9): partners, not enemies

Big Four platforms (Deloitte GlobalAdvantage — 5,300+ clients, also white-labels Equus; EY Mobility Pathway; KPMG LINK Go; PwC myMobility) and data houses (Mercer Mobility — "global standard-setter for expat package data"; AIRINC; plus tax specialists GTN, Certino, and Vialto, the PwC carve-out).

Two things matter here:

1. **EY Mobility Pathway is described as "the closest incumbent description to ReloPass's own positioning"** — connects staff, vendors, managers and employees on one platform. The difference is buyer (enterprise, expertise-gated) and price. That's the gap you exploit, not a reason to copy them.
2. **The objection you *will* face**, verbatim from KPMG's positioning: **"expertise + tech vs. pure software vendors."** Buyers in this space distrust software-only players on high-stakes compliance. Your answer to that objection is your entire moat: *provable, evidence-linked, independently-verified* correctness. If you can't show the proof, you lose to "the Big Four says so."

**Read:** Mercer/AIRINC (cost data), GTN/Certino (tax) are **integration partners**, not competitors — data you need or substitute, not fight. Treat this tier as a supplier shelf.

## 6. Tier 5 — Adjacent (29): where the real threat lives

This tier splits three ways:

**(a) The absorption threat — EOR/global-employment platforms bolting on mobility.** This is the existential risk.
- **Deel — "the biggest absorption threat. Deel Mobility now open to non-Deel customers. Ranks #1–2 in G2's Relocation Management category."** An EOR that owns the employment relationship, has enormous distribution, and is now selling immigration/relocation *to everyone*. If any single competitor kills ReloPass, it's this pattern.
- Remote (launched "Remote MCP" — AI agents get live payroll/compliance data), Pebl/ex-Velocity Global ("AI-first EOR, 185+ countries, 'Alfie'"), Papaya Global (dedicated global immigration, 160+ countries; May 2026 partnership with Fragomen), Multiplier, Oyster, Atlas HXM ("Sponsor & Relocate Talent" — most explicit mobility position among EORs), WorkMotion, Safeguard, G-P. Rippling has no immigration product *yet* but "its spend primitive maps onto relocation expense and lump sum" — a latent entrant.

**(b) The record-of-truth — HCM suites you integrate INTO, not against.** Workday ("no relocation module — mobility vendors integrate into Workday; owns the HR system of record"), **SAP SuccessFactors** ("the only major HCM with a real mobility object — home/host, assignment dates, repatriation — but no vendor or cost coordination"), Oracle, Dayforce, Personio (your ICP overlap), and **HiBob (ships an MCP integration for agentic access to people data — an easy data source for an AI-native mobility layer)**. These are distribution and data rails, not competitors.

**(c) The supply layer — housing, movers, marketplaces you coordinate.** AltoVita (7M+ properties), Blueground ("Blueground for Business" — already owns the buyer at Google/Uber/EY/Tesla), Nestpick, ReloQuest (1M+ options, 190+ countries), Shyft (household-goods moves, used by Graebel), plus talent marketplaces (Global Sponsor Hub, Relocate.me) as upstream lead sources.

**Read:** Fear (a), ride (b) and (c). The coordination-layer thesis only works if you're the *neutral* layer that plugs the supply (housing/movers/tax/immigration) into the HR record-of-truth (Workday/HiBob/Personio via MCP). EORs can't be neutral — they're selling you the employment relationship. That neutrality is a structural advantage you should name explicitly.

## 7. What ReloPass is NOT delivering that competitors are (the honest gaps)

1. **Scale and social proof** — Localyze (10k+), Jobbatical (17k+), Settly (5k+). You're pre-traction; they have logo walls.
2. **Mature HRIS/tool integrations** — premote and Settly have deep integration stories; yours are thin. In this category, integrations *are* the go-to-market.
3. **Filing-execution depth** — Fragomen/Envoy/Manifest OS actually assemble and file petitions. You surface requirements; you don't (and shouldn't yet) execute filings.
4. **Proprietary cost/hardship/tax data** — Mercer and AIRINC own the numbers. You'll need to license or partner, not build.
5. **Physical move execution & DSP networks** — the RMCs' real asset. Correctly a "no" for you (low-margin), but it's a capability gap buyers will ask about.
6. **Enterprise trust / "expertise + tech"** — the KPMG objection. Your only answer is provable accuracy.

Naming these honestly matters: a credible North Star has to say what you deliberately *don't* build and how you cover the gap (partner, integrate, or ignore).

## 8. Does this confirm the moat? Yes — but only the reframed one

The competitor set **kills the naive moat** ("a relocation platform," "AI-native," "multi-corridor") — 158 companies already have those. It **confirms the real moat**, which is the intersection almost nobody occupies:

- **The buyer nobody serves.** Tier 1 targets enterprise HR/mobility teams; Tier 2 targets mobility managers and RMC procurement; Tier 3 targets law firms and enterprise; Tier 4 is Big-Four enterprise; Tier 5 EORs target their own payroll customers. **The SME HR generalist handling an occasional high-stakes move is structurally ignored by all five tiers.** Your North Star's "mobility manager at an SME barely exists" is validated by the entire dataset.
- **The proof nobody sells.** Everyone claims AI and accuracy; **nobody publishes verifiable, evidence-linked, independently-checked correctness with freshness monitoring.** In a market where the buyer objection is literally "expertise + tech beats software," *provable* correctness is the one wedge that answers it — and it's the one you've already built the machinery for (the P4-01…04 pipeline + RAG eval harness).

---

## 9. Differentiation — five defensible angles

1. **Own the SME generalist.** Purpose-build for the non-specialist handling their first/second move — the buyer every other tier is structurally built to skip. This is a positioning moat money can't shortcut (focus is the part incumbents can't copy without abandoning their enterprise base).
2. **Sell the proof, not the AI.** Make accuracy a *visible product*: every flagged requirement carries its official source, an evidence quote, a freshness date, and an independently-verified confidence. Publish your eval scores. Nobody else can, because nobody else runs the verifier — it directly defeats the "expertise + tech" objection.
3. **Be the neutral coordination layer.** Integrate the supply (housing: AltoVita/Blueground/Nestpick; movers: Shyft; tax: GTN/Certino; data: Mercer/AIRINC; filing: VisaHQ) and the record-of-truth (Workday/HiBob/Personio/Remote via MCP). EORs and RMCs *can't* be neutral — they're selling you employment or the move. You can.
4. **Corridor depth as the wedge, breadth through the engine.** Win one corridor visibly (FR→NO, provably correct), then scale corridors through the same eval-gated engine — so breadth compounds trust instead of diluting it (see North Star v2).
5. **AI-native on the one thing that's a trust product.** Not "AI-native" as a slogan — AI-native specifically on *maintained correctness* (the maintenance engine, not a copied snapshot). That's the layer that rots for everyone else and stays fresh for you.

## 10. Threats to watch, ranked

1. **Deel Mobility (absorption).** #1–2 in G2 Relocation, now open to non-Deel customers, enormous distribution. Countered only by neutrality + SME focus + provable accuracy — not by feature parity.
2. **Down-market AI-native immigration entrants.** Manifest OS ($60M), LegalOS (YC W26), Casium, Alma, Gale Visa — well-funded, fast, and moving toward the employer/SME buyer. They own immigration depth; you must own the *whole move + the generalist* before they broaden.
3. **Permitree / Centuro feasibility engines.** Already shipping the "surface the unknowns" experience. Your edge over them is verification + coverage of the whole move for the SME buyer, not the feasibility check itself.
4. **Localyze.** Closest venture-backed European analogue; if anyone is building your exact product for a similar buyer, it's them. Watch their SME motion.

## 11. Partner-don't-compete map (the integration strategy)

| Need | Partner with | Not build |
|---|---|---|
| Cost / hardship data | Mercer, AIRINC | proprietary COLA data |
| Shadow payroll / tax | GTN, Certino, Vialto | a tax engine |
| Corporate housing | AltoVita, Blueground, Nestpick, ReloQuest | a housing marketplace |
| Household-goods moves | Shyft | a move-logistics network |
| Visa filing execution | VisaHQ (API-first) | a filing/attorney operation |
| HR record-of-truth / data | Workday, HiBob, Personio, Remote (all shipping MCP) | an HCM |

Every row you partner is a capability gap closed *without* becoming a low-margin services or data business — and each keeps you the neutral layer EORs and RMCs can't be.

## Bottom line

The market is enormous, crowded, and already "AI-native and multi-corridor" — so none of that wins. The two things that do win are the two things almost no one has: **the SME HR generalist as the buyer, and provable, maintained correctness as the product.** Everything else on the shelf — data, tax, housing, movers, HCM, even filing — is something to *ride*, not fight. The one thing to genuinely fear is EOR absorption (Deel), and the answer to it is structural: be the neutral, provably-correct coordination layer for the buyer they don't serve.
