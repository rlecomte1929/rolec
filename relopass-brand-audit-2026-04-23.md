## FINDINGS (sorted 🔴 → 🟠 → 🟡)

---

### 🔴 SITE-WIDE — Differentiation statement absent

**Current:** *(not stated on any page)*  
**Issue:** The core competitive wedge — "Not an agency. Not a marketplace. Not an HR add-on." — does not appear anywhere across the five pages. A buyer who lands on any page has no way to understand what category ReloPass is replacing.  
**Rewrite:** Add to Homepage (between System overview and Trust) and Why ReloPass (as its own section):  
> "Not an agency. Not a marketplace. Not an HR add-on. The coordination layer across HR, employees, and providers."  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #5 Concreteness

---

### 🔴 SITE-WIDE — "Workspace" used instead of "operating layer" / "infrastructure"

**Current:** "Every relocation in one workspace" (Platform hero) · "Run relocation as one process" (Homepage hero) · "one case per relocation, activity tied to the case" (Homepage)  
**Issue:** "Workspace" frames ReloPass as a collaboration tool (Notion, Airtable register). "Operating layer" and "infrastructure" are the correct category signals. Neither appears on any page.  
**Rewrite (Platform hero):**  
> "The operating layer for your relocation workflow."  
**Rewrite (Homepage hero):**  
> "Structure how you run relocation. Cases, policy, providers, and status — one operating layer."  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #3 Tone

---

### 🔴 SITE-WIDE — Brand promise is one-third present

**Current:** Visibility is implied throughout. "Compliant" and "on-time" never appear.  
**Issue:** The full brand promise is "Every relocation case is visible, compliant, on-time." Compliance exposure is the sharpest buyer fear — it is also the strongest differentiator from spreadsheets and agencies. Its absence makes the site sound like a tracking tool, not an infrastructure product.  
**Rewrite (Homepage problem section, add to existing):**  
> "Missed compliance steps and late visa filings aren't tracked until it's too late."  
**Rewrite (Feature card / how it works):**  
> "Policy applied at case creation. Audit-ready close on every case."  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #5 Concreteness

---

### 🟠 Homepage — Hero undersells category; problem stakes too thin

**Current:** "Run relocation as one process. Cases, documents, providers, and status in one place. Less thread-chasing and spreadsheet glue work."  
**Issue:** "One process" is a generic workflow claim. "Thread-chasing and spreadsheet glue work" names friction but not failure consequences — no compliance risk, no dropped handoffs, no visibility gap. The problem stakes section lists features ("Case-based workflow", "Policy alignment") instead of buyer pain.  
**Rewrite (hero sub):**  
> "Cases, documents, providers, and policy in one system of record. No manual status chasing. No missed compliance steps."  
**Rewrite (problem stakes header):**  
> "Relocation still breaks in the same places."  
**Voice check:** #3 Tone, #5 Concreteness, #6 Sentence discipline

---

### 🟠 Homepage — Missing four required arc sections

**Current:** Hero → Light problem framing → Feature bullets → CTA  
**Issue:** The required homepage arc has 8 sections. The following are absent: *(3) System overview as operating layer*, *(5) Product proof — annotated UI*, *(6) Differentiation*, *(7) Trust — policy execution / auditability*. The current structure jumps from a thin problem statement directly to feature bullets, skipping the narrative beats that move a buyer from "interesting" to "book a demo."  
**Fix:** Insert between the problem section and footer CTA:
1. **System overview block** — "One system. Cases at the centre. Policy, providers, documents, and status connected to each one."  
2. **Annotated UI** — Add callout text to the existing product screenshot explaining what the buyer is looking at (case status, provider task routing, compliance checkpoint).  
3. **Differentiation block** — "Not an agency. Not a marketplace. Not an HR add-on."  
4. **Trust row** — "Policy applied at case creation. Exceptions surfaced automatically. Audit-ready close."  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #2 Audience fit, #5 Concreteness

### 🟠 Platform page — "Every relocation in one workspace" misstates the category

**Current:** "Every relocation in one workspace. Case, documents, providers, and progress in one view."  
**Issue:** "Workspace" signals collaboration tool, not infrastructure. The Platform page is where buyers form their mental model of the product — it must lead with architecture, not with UI convenience.  
**Rewrite:**  
> "Every relocation runs on one structured case. Documents, providers, and policy in one system — not split across tools and inboxes."  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #6 Sentence discipline

---

### 🟠 Platform page — Module breakdown and dual-perspective section absent

**Current:** Case overview, Documents, Service providers — listed as three labels with one-line descriptions.  
**Issue:** The required Platform arc requires each module to have: label + headline + 2-sentence function description. The dual-perspective section ("Same case. Two structured views.") is entirely absent. Without it, a buyer cannot see the product through their own role.  
**Fix:** Expand each module to function-first descriptions, e.g.:  
> **Documents** — "Requirements and files tied to the case, not scattered in email. HR sees what's missing; employees see what to submit."  
Add bridging line: "Same case. Two structured views."  
**Voice check:** #2 Audience fit, #5 Concreteness

---

### 🟠 Why ReloPass — Category framing section and contrast blocks absent

**Current:** "What is different" section covers "Case at the center" and "Less chasing" — 2 columns, no structured before/after contrast.  
**Issue:** The required arc for Why ReloPass requires: *(4) Category framing* ("A new category: mobility operations") and *(5) Contrast blocks* (Without ReloPass vs. With ReloPass, matched rows). Neither exists. The page describes operational benefits without ever claiming the category.  
**Rewrite (add section after "What is different"):**  
> **A new category: mobility operations.**  
> "ReloPass is not a better version of what you already use. It's the operating layer that replaces the coordination gap between your HR system, your employees, and your providers."  
**Contrast block example:**  
| Without ReloPass | With ReloPass |
|---|---|
| Status lives in vendor emails | Status lives on the case |
| Compliance steps tracked in spreadsheets | Policy applied at case creation |
| Follow-up is manual | Exceptions surfaced automatically |  
**Voice check:** #1 Category framing, #4 Forbidden registers, #5 Concreteness

### 🟠 Why ReloPass — Closing line is defensive

**Current:** "Less friction, not more software for its own sake."  
**Issue:** "For its own sake" is a defensive qualifier — it implies an unspoken objection ("isn't this just more software?"). It weakens the close. End on operational strength.

## TOP 3 PRIORITIES

**1. Add the differentiation statement to Homepage and Why ReloPass.**  
"Not an agency. Not a marketplace. Not an HR add-on. The coordination layer across HR, employees, and providers." This is the single highest-leverage copy gap on the site. A buyer who has seen competitors cannot tell from the current site what category they're being asked to choose. One block, two pages, immediate positioning lift.

**2. Swap "workspace" for "operating layer" and add the compliance angle to the brand promise.**  
The word "workspace" appears in the Platform hero — the most important positioning real estate on that page. Replace it with "operating layer." Separately, "compliant" is in the brand promise but nowhere on the site. Add "audit-ready close" and "policy applied at case creation" to the Homepage and How it works. These two changes alone move the score on Category clarity and Brand promise from 🔴 to 🟡.

**3. Build out the Why ReloPass page with the three missing sections.**  
The page has the best hero on the site ("Relocation fails in the handoffs") but then fails to close the argument. Adding the three-category breakdown of why current tools fail, the category claim ("A new category: mobility operations"), and structured contrast blocks turns this page from a problem-naming page into a category-winning page. This is where motivated buyers go to confirm their decision — give them the argument.
