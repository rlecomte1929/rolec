# Narrative arcs by content type

Use this file when checking whether a piece of content is structured correctly (Step 4 of the audit). Each arc is the required sequence of jobs — if sections are missing, out of order, or doing the wrong job, flag them.

---

## Website: Homepage

The homepage must move a first-time HR/mobility buyer from category awareness to enough understanding to book a demo.

1. **Hero** — Category statement + buyer value + two CTAs (primary: Book a demo; secondary: See the platform)
2. **Problem stakes** — The coordination failure made vivid: email threads, spreadsheets, fragmented vendors, no visibility, compliance risk
3. **System overview** — ReloPass as the operating layer: cases, timelines, documents, providers, policy controls, status
4. **How it works** — 3–4 operational steps (create case → apply policy → coordinate → monitor to close)
5. **Product proof** — Annotated UI showing workflow state, case view, or provider coordination
6. **Differentiation** — Explicitly: not an agency, not a marketplace, not an HR add-on
7. **Trust** — Operational logic, auditability, policy execution, corridor intelligence
8. **Final CTA** — Restate the offer; invite conversation about their relocation operations

**Common failures:** Hero uses employee-facing ("your journey") rather than buyer-facing language. Problem section describes features instead of pain. Product screenshots are decorative rather than explanatory.

---

## Website: Platform page

The platform page must give a buyer a concrete mental model of what the product contains and how it connects.

1. **Platform hero** — One-line architecture statement + CTA
2. **System architecture overview** — Visual or prose explanation of how modules connect around the case
3. **Module-by-module breakdown** — For each: cases / timelines / documents / providers / policy controls / visibility. Each module: label + headline + 2-sentence function + supporting UI
4. **Dual perspective** — What HR sees vs. what the employee sees. Bridging line: "Same case. Two structured views."
5. **CTA** — 30-minute walkthrough framing + Book a demo

**Common failures:** Module descriptions read as feature lists rather than operational outcomes. The case as the central unit is not made clear. Employee view is shown without framing it as a consequence of good HR operations.

## Website: Why ReloPass

This page must win the category argument. It answers: why not keep using what you have?

1. **Hero** — Lead with the problem, not the solution. ("Relocation fails in the handoffs.")
2. **The coordination problem** — Frame relocation as a coordination failure first, not a complexity problem
3. **Why current tools fail** — Three categories: agencies (no visibility), spreadsheets (breaks at scale), HR add-ons (not built for this)
4. **Category framing** — "A new category: mobility operations." ReloPass is not a better version of what exists.
5. **Contrast blocks** — Without ReloPass (fragmented) vs. With ReloPass (structured). Match rows precisely.
6. **CTA** — Invite conversation about their specific process

**Common failures:** Page is defensive rather than assertive. Category framing is soft ("we're different because…"). Contrast blocks are unbalanced or vague on the "with" side.

---

## Website: How it works

This page must remove cognitive friction. Buyers who cannot see how adoption works defer.

1. **Hero** — Short orientation statement (max 2 sentences). "Four steps from fragmented to structured."
2. **Step sequence** — Maximum 4 steps. Each: number + headline + 2-sentence description + supporting UI
3. **Optional: setup timeline** — 3 phases (configure / first cases / full operation) with durations
4. **CTA** — "Start with one corridor." + timeframe to operational ("Most teams are fully operational within two weeks.")

**Common failures:** More than 4 steps (cognitive overload). Steps describe concepts rather than actions. No product UI per step. No timeframe for adoption.

---

## Website: Get started / Contact

This page must convert interest into a meeting with zero friction.

1. **Hero** — Buyer-focused headline. Use approved line: "Three ways in. Book a demo, sign in, or create an account."
2. **Demo path** — Form (name, work email, company, relocation volume, optional challenge). Trust microcopy below CTA: 30-minute walkthrough, no commitment.
3. **What the demo covers** — 5 bullet points showing what the buyer will actually see
4. **Secondary paths** — Sign in card + See the platform card
5. **Optional trust row** — 3–4 operational proof statements (no logos if not yet available)

**Common failures:** Form asks for too much. No trust microcopy. No "what you'll see" section. Primary CTA is generic ("Submit" or "Send").

## Pitch deck / Investor deck

Aimed at investors. Must establish market size, category creation opportunity, and business model. Different from the sales deck — problem/solution framing is investor-grade, not buyer-grade.

1. **Cover** — Company name + one-line category descriptor. "ReloPass — Global mobility infrastructure."
2. **Problem** — Market-level: global mobility is a $X billion market running on manual coordination. Quantify the cost of failure.
3. **Why now** — What has changed in the market that makes this the right moment
4. **Solution** — ReloPass as the operating layer. Category claim.
5. **Product** — 2–3 slides showing the core workflow: case → policy → coordination → compliant close
6. **Business model** — How ReloPass charges. Simple, clear.
7. **Market** — TAM/SAM/SOM or narrative market sizing. Who are the buyers? How many?
8. **Traction** — Pilots, design partners, letters of intent, case metrics if available
9. **Team** — Why this team for this problem
10. **Ask** — How much, for what milestones

**Common failures:** Category claim buried in slide 3 or 4 instead of leading. Product slides are UI screenshots without workflow narration. Team slide positioned before traction. Ask is vague on use of funds.

## Sales deck / Product presentation

Aimed at HR/mobility buyers in a demo or discovery context. The buyer needs to see themselves in the product.

1. **Cover** — "ReloPass" + short qualifier: "The operating layer for your relocation workflow."
2. **Their problem** — Name the buyer's specific pain before talking about ReloPass. 2–3 slides max. Use operational language: dropped handoffs, no visibility, compliance exposure.
3. **What ReloPass does** — One-slide architecture: the case at the centre, modules connecting to it
4. **Product walkthrough** — Guided by their use case. Show: case creation → policy application → provider coordination → compliant close. Annotate each screen.
5. **Their workflow in ReloPass** — A concrete example using their corridor type (e.g., domestic / international / L-visa). Make it feel specific.
6. **Implementation** — How they get started. 3-phase timeline (configure / pilot / full operation). Week-level specificity.
7. **Next steps** — One clear action. "Let's configure one corridor together."

**Common failures:** Deck opens with company history or investor-grade market sizing (wrong audience). Product walkthrough uses a generic demo case rather than their corridor type. No implementation slide — buyers need to know how they get from today to running cases. Next steps are vague.

## One-pager / Leave-behind

A single page left after a meeting. Must work without context or explanation. Buyer should be able to share it internally.

Structure (in order, top to bottom):
1. **Header** — Logo + one-line category: "Global mobility infrastructure"
2. **Problem statement** — 2 sentences. What breaks without ReloPass.
3. **What ReloPass does** — 3–4 lines max. System-of-record framing.
4. **How it works** — 3 steps in a visual strip or numbered list
5. **Key capabilities** — 4–6 bullets. Operational outcomes, not features.
6. **Why it's different** — 3 contrast lines: "Not an agency. Not a marketplace. The operating layer."
7. **Contact / CTA** — One action: "Book a 30-minute walkthrough."

**Common failures:** Too much text — one-pagers must survive a 10-second scan. Category claim not in the header. Features listed instead of outcomes. No single clear CTA.

## Email (outbound / follow-up)

1. **Subject line** — Specific and operational. Not "Following up" or "Quick question." Example: "How [Company] runs relocation today — and what changes"
2. **Opening** — 1 sentence. Reference something specific about their situation.
3. **Problem** — 1–2 sentences. Name the coordination failure you believe they have.
4. **ReloPass in one line** — What it does. Stay in the system-of-record register.
5. **Evidence** — One concrete thing: a feature, a case type, a corridor, a metric.
6. **CTA** — Single, specific, low-friction. "Would a 20-minute walkthrough next week be useful?"

**Common failures:** Subject line is generic. Opening doesn't reference their situation. ReloPass described as a product category rather than a solution to their named problem. Multiple CTAs at the end.
