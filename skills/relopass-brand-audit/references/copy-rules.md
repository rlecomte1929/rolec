# Copy rules and substitution table

Use this file when rewriting flagged copy (Step 5 of the audit). It covers the forbidden registers, defensive qualifiers, and direct swap guidance.

---

## Forbidden registers — flag and rewrite any of these

### Startup hype
Words/phrases that signal over-promise and undermine the calm, precise brand register.

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "revolutionary" | Hype. Makes a claim the copy doesn't support. |
| "game-changing" | Hype. Vague and overused. |
| "disrupt" / "disruptive" | Category jargon, not a buyer benefit. |
| "supercharge" | Energy-drink register. Wrong for infrastructure. |
| "unlock" | Vague metaphor. Replace with the specific outcome. |
| "next-gen" / "next-generation" | Meaningless without specification. |
| "transform" (as a brand promise) | Too large a claim for a workflow tool. |
| "reimagine" | Consulting register. Replace with the concrete change. |

### Consulting filler
Words used to signal seriousness but that carry no information.

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "synergies" | Empty. Always replace with the specific outcome. |
| "best-in-class" | Unverifiable. Replace with a specific differentiator. |
| "leverage" (as a verb) | Overused filler. Use "use" or name the specific action. |
| "end-to-end" (as filler) | Means nothing without specifying the endpoints. |
| "holistic" | Vague. Replace with the specific scope. |
| "bespoke" | Consulting preciousness. Use "configured to your policy." |

### Lifestyle-relocation language
Pulls perception toward relocation service / consumer product. Incompatible with B2B infrastructure positioning.

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "journey" | Consumer framing. Signals employee companion, not HR control system. |
| "your relocation journey" | Combines wrong audience ("your") with wrong register ("journey"). |
| "new adventure" | Consumer lifestyle copy. |
| "exciting chapter" | Consumer lifestyle copy. |
| "pack your bags" | Consumer lifestyle copy. |
| "new home" / "new city" (as primary frame) | Employee-facing, not buyer-facing. |
| Travel imagery (planes, passports, suitcases) | Visual register undermines infrastructure positioning. |

### Generic SaaS filler
Words that describe every B2B tool and therefore describe nothing.

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "powerful platform" | Every SaaS product claims this. Replace with a specific capability. |
| "seamless experience" | Meaningless. Name what specifically is not interrupted. |
| "robust" | Vague strength claim. Replace with a specific reliability or depth claim. |
| "all-in-one" | Breadth claim that implies compromise. Use "one system of record for…" instead. |
| "intuitive" | Every product claims this. Replace with what the user can do without training. |
| "scalable" (as standalone claim) | Replace with what it scales to and how. |

### Defensive qualifiers
Phrases that imply you're defending against an unspoken objection. They weaken every sentence they appear in.

| Forbidden | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "real relocations" | Implies a question about whether ReloPass handles real vs. demo cases. Remove the qualifier. |
| "actual workflows" | Same pattern. Remove. |
| "true visibility" | Same pattern. Just say "visibility." |
| "genuine infrastructure" | Same pattern. Just say "infrastructure." |
| "proper system of record" | Same pattern. Just say "system of record." |

## Copy substitution table

Direct swap guidance for the most common failures.

| Current (wrong) | Replacement direction |
|---|---|
| "Your relocation journey" | "Your relocation operations" — or restructure the sentence entirely |
| "Seamless relocation" | "Structured relocation execution" |
| "All-in-one relocation platform" | "One system of record for cases, documents, providers, and timelines" |
| "Simplify the journey" | "Reduce coordination overhead" / "Replace manual tracking with structured workflows" |
| "Support every move" | "Track every case from policy to completion" |
| "Powerful relocation platform" | Describe the specific capability — e.g., "Policy-driven case management" |
| "Robust solution" | Describe the specific outcome — e.g., "Audit-ready close on every case" |
| "Real relocations" | Remove qualifier. State the claim directly. |
| "Seamless coordination" | "Coordinated through one system" / "Tasks routed to the right party at the right time" |
| "Game-changing" | Remove. Replace with a concrete operational claim. |
| "Exciting new chapter" | Remove entirely. Not buyer-facing copy. |
| "Help HR teams manage relocation" | "Give HR teams visibility and control over every relocation case" |
| "One platform for all your relocation needs" | "One system for cases, timelines, documents, providers, and status" |
| "We partner with you" | "Configured to your policy and corridors" |
| "End-to-end relocation solution" | "From case creation to compliant close" + specify what the system handles |
| "Simplify your relocation process" | "Replace manual coordination with structured workflows" |
| "ReloPass helps HR teams" | "ReloPass gives HR teams" (gives = outcome; helps = vague support) |

## Operational language to prefer

These phrases are on-brand and should be drawn on when rewriting:

**System and infrastructure:**
- "operating layer for global mobility"
- "system of record for relocation execution"
- "policy-driven system"
- "coordination layer"
- "structured case"

**Outcomes for HR buyers:**
- "visibility across every open case"
- "policy-applied at case creation"
- "audit-ready close"
- "no manual status chasing"
- "exceptions surfaced automatically"
- "structured task routing"
- "on-time, compliant execution"

**Outcomes for employees (secondary):**
- "a structured path through their relocation"
- "clear next steps"
- "document requirements in one place"
- "no chasing HR for updates"

**Differentiation:**
- "not an agency"
- "not a marketplace"
- "not an HR add-on"
- "the coordination layer across HR, employees, and providers"
- "built for mobility operators"

## Sentence discipline rules

Apply these to every rewrite:

1. **One idea per sentence.** Split compound sentences.
2. **Active voice.** "The system routes tasks" not "Tasks are routed by the system."
3. **Remove throat-clearing openers.** Don't start with "With ReloPass, you can…" — just say what it does.
4. **No stacked adjectives.** "Policy-driven workflow" yes. "Powerful, seamless, policy-driven workflow" no.
5. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "30-minute walkthrough" beats "quick walkthrough." "4-step workflow" beats "simple workflow."
6. **Specific nouns beat vague nouns.** "Cases, timelines, documents, providers" beats "everything you need."
7. **End on strength.** The last word of a sentence or section carries the most weight. Don't end on a qualifier or a filler word.
