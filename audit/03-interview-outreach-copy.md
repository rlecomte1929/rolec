# Wave-5 Outreach Copy Library

**Purpose:** Book 20-min research conversations with in-house HR/mobility leads. These messages are NOT sales pitches — they open a discovery conversation.

**Before sending:** run the screener (audit/03-interview-screener.md) to confirm the prospect is an in-house buyer, not a vendor or consultant.

**The one rule:** never mention the product in the opening message. Lead with their pain, ask for their time, stop.

---

## Persona 1 — Stretched HR Director (200–500 employee company)

*Matching signals: "I'm the only one handling this", "We only do 5-10 moves a year", "It's not my full-time job but it consumes a lot of my time". Find them: HR Director, VP People, Head of HR at Series B–D tech companies or mid-market professional services firms.*

### Variant A — LinkedIn DM (general EU)

> Hi [Name], I noticed you manage HR across [Company] — I'm doing research on how HR Directors at companies your size handle international relocations when there's no dedicated mobility team.
>
> Would you be open to a 20-min call? Purely research — I'm trying to understand where the real friction is before an employee is set up at the destination.
>
> Happy to share what I'm hearing from others if it's useful.

**Word count:** 64  
**Personalisation:** [Name], [Company]  
**Opener anchor:** "no dedicated mobility team" — the defining pain of Persona 1 (BRAIN-3B: "it's 10% of my job description but 50% of my stress")

## Persona 2 — Head of Global Mobility (800–2,000 employee company)

*Matching signals: "I'm the first person in this role", "We have a spreadsheet but it's not holding up", "I need to build a proper process". Find them: Head of Global Mobility, Global Mobility Manager, Mobility Lead — often recently hired (< 12 months in role) at scaling tech or professional services companies.*

### Variant A — LinkedIn DM (first hire in role)

> Hi [Name], I'm doing research on how newly-hired mobility leads build their first structured process — specifically the gap between inheriting a spreadsheet on day one and having something defensible to show upward six months later.
>
> Would you be open to 20 minutes? No agenda beyond understanding what the first months in your role actually look like.

**Word count:** 60  
**Personalisation:** [Name]  
**Opener anchor:** "inheriting a spreadsheet on day one" — the exact Persona 2 trigger (BRAIN-3B: "She inherited a function that ran on spreadsheets, personal relationships, and institutional knowledge that lived in the previous coordinator's head")

*Matching signals: "I manage our HR systems", "I'm spending too much time chasing people for documents", "We're running more moves this year than last". ## Usage notes

### Which variant to use when

| Persona | Scenario | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Persona 1 | French-market target, French company name or FR HQ | Variant B (French) |
| Persona 1 | All other EU targets | Variant A |
| Persona 2 | LinkedIn shows they were hired < 12 months ago into a new mobility role | Variant A (first hire) |
| Persona 2 | LinkedIn shows they've been in a mobility role > 12 months | Variant B (process-builder) |
| Persona 3 | Email is available (company domain found via Hunter/LinkedIn) | Cold email Variant A |
| Persona 3 | Email not available | Adapt Variant A into a LinkedIn DM |

### Filling in the [BRACKETS]

- **[Name] / [Prénom]:** Always use first name only. Never "Dear Mr./Ms." — too formal.
- **[Company]:** Use the company name from LinkedIn, not the LinkedIn abbreviation. "Lhoist" not "LHOIST S.A."
- **[week/next week]:** Pick whichever applies; be specific. "this week" is more urgent; "next week" is less pressuring.

### What to do after they reply "yes"

1. Send the screener (audit/03-interview-screener.md) — either as a Google Form link or embedded as 2-3 questions in your reply.
2. Once qualified, suggest a time and confirm with a calendar link.
3. Before the call, re-read their LinkedIn to identify which Persona 1-3 they are and which hypothesis persona probes (Q9-10 from the guide) are worth including.

### The most common reply you'll get (and how to respond)

> "Sounds interesting, what's this for exactly?"

> "I'm trying to understand the real friction in international relocations at companies your size — specifically where coordination breaks down. I'm not selling anything at this stage; I'm building research that will eventually shape a tool, but right now I just want to understand the problem from people who live it. Happy to share what I'm learning if you find it useful."
