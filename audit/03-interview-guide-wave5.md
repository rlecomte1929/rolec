# Wave-5 Discovery Call Guide (Mom Test)

**Purpose:** Run a 30–45 min discovery conversation that produces concrete, datable, past-behavior evidence — not opinions. Every answer should tell you about their real life, not their imagined future.

**Mom Test principle:** Ask about the past, not the future. About their life, not your idea. Never pitch; never lead. A question that makes them say "yes, definitely" is a bad question. A question that makes them tell a story is a good one.

**Who this is for:** Mid-market HR Directors, Heads of Global Mobility, and HR Ops Managers (Personas 1–3 from BRAIN-3B). Personas 4–5 probe questions are embedded in Section 2.

**Before the call:** Read the screener response. Confirm they are in-house (not a vendor, RMC, or DSP). Have the BRAIN-3B persona card open so you know which archetype you're likely talking to.

---

## Section 1 — Opening and context (5 min)

*Goal: establish rapport, understand their current situation, and confirm the persona match. Do NOT mention ReloPass yet.*

**Q1. "To make sure I use your time well — can you walk me through what your current role covers, specifically around international mobility or relocation?"**

> Interviewer note: You want to hear whether they own it directly or whether it lands on their desk as one of many responsibilities. "Stretched HR Director" says "it's 10% of my job but it feels like 50%." "Head of Global Mobility" says "it's my whole function." If they say "we outsource it entirely to [RMC]," thank them and close early — they're not the signal you need. Grade A: specific ownership language. Grade B: general ownership with vague hand-offs. Grade C: "We don't really do relocations."

**Q2. "How many international moves did you handle in the last 12 months — roughly?"**

> Interviewer note: Validates the ICP. 5–10 moves = Persona 1 territory. 20–100 moves = Persona 2/3. Under 5 with no pipeline = too early. Over 200 = probably enterprise with a dedicated RMC contract. Exact number matters less than the scale-signal.

**Q3. "Who else in your company is typically involved when a relocation opens — who touches it from your side?"**

> Interviewer note: Reveals whether there's a coordination problem. Persona 1 answer: "just me, with some support from our Office Manager." Persona 2 answer: "me plus finance, the line manager, sometimes legal for work permits." Persona 5 signal: "I have to loop in our global mobility team in HQ, which adds a week every time."

## Section 2 — Pain excavation (20 min)

*Goal: surface real friction with specifics — time lost, cost, emotional stakes. Every question anchors in past behavior.*

**Q4. "Think about the last relocation case you handled from start to finish. Walk me through it — what was the first thing you did when the case opened, and what was the last thing before you closed it?"**

> Interviewer note: This is the most important question. Do NOT interrupt. Listen for: manual steps (email chains, spreadsheets, PDF attachments), moment where things fell through (missed permit, late vendor, employee confusion), and who they had to chase. The story structure reveals where the real pain is. Victoria @ NBIM said: "Too many contacts and handovers, lack of single ownership." Grade A: specific timeline with named handover moments and friction points. Grade B: general description with emotional frustration. Grade C: "it was fine, pretty smooth."

**Q5. "What was the most stressful part of that case? When did you feel like things could go wrong?"**

> Interviewer note: Surfaces the emotional peak of their experience. You're looking for: work permit risk ("if the authorization came back late, they couldn't start"), housing crisis ("the employee arrived and the apartment fell through"), tax exposure ("we found out afterwards there was a PE risk"). These are the signals that the cost of failure is high. Grade A: specific named risk with a clear consequence. Grade B: general stress about timelines. Grade C: "I don't know, it was fine."

**Q6. "How long did it take you from 'we've decided to relocate this person' to 'they're legally set up and working at the destination'? And was that typical?"**

> Interviewer note: Quantifies the time cost. You want a number: "3 months", "4-6 weeks if we're lucky". Then: "and what was your involvement in that time?" You're looking for the hidden labour — the hours of coordination that are invisible in a salary line. Grade A: specific number + estimate of personal hours invested. Grade B: "a few months, I was quite involved." Grade C: "I don't really track it."

**Q7. "Has a relocation ever gone wrong — an error, a delay, something that created a problem for the employee or the company?"**

> Interviewer note: The war story. If they say no: "Nothing even small?" Most HR people have at least one. The story tells you the severity of failure in their context. If they won't share, ask: "In general, what's the worst that can happen with an international move for an HR team?" Grade A: named incident with concrete consequence (penalty, employee complaint, legal exposure). Grade B: near-miss or general near-miss description. Grade C: no incident and no imagination of failure.

**Q8. "How do you currently keep track of where each case stands — what's your system?"**

> Interviewer note: This is the tech-stack probe. You want to know: spreadsheet (chaos), email (worse), shared folder (fragile), nothing formal ("I keep it in my head"), or existing tool. If they mention a tool: "How much of the process does it cover? What does it leave out?" Spreadsheet/email = high ReloPass fit. Established platform = lower urgency. Grade A: describes a manual or fragile system with specific gaps. Grade B: uses something but acknowledges limitations. Grade C: "We have a system that works well."

**— Persona 4 (CHRO) probe — use if they have C-suite orientation or reference board/finance:**

**Q9. "Have you ever been asked by your CEO, CFO, or board for data on your mobility program — costs, compliance status, where your people are?"**

> Interviewer note: Validates the Persona 4 (CHRO) hypothesis: the trigger is a board or finance question about mobility risk. If yes: "What happened? Were you able to answer it?" The inability to answer quickly is the pain. If no: "Could you answer it today if you needed to?" If they hesitate: that's the signal. Grade A: specific board/finance question they couldn't answer cleanly. Grade B: general acknowledgement that the data isn't visible. Grade C: "Yes, we have full visibility."

**— Persona 5 (Regional HRBP) probe — use if they mention managing HR across multiple countries or reporting to a global function:**

**Q10. "When a local business unit initiates a cross-border move, how does it reach you? Do you ever find out about moves that were already in motion before you were looped in?"**

> Interviewer note: Validates the Persona 5 (Regional HRBP) hypothesis: the pain is late discovery of moves that create compliance exposure. If yes: "Can you give me an example?" Grade A: specific late-discovery incident with compliance consequence. Grade B: "it happens sometimes" without a specific example. Grade C: "No, we have a clear process for that."

## Section 3 — Signal questions (10 min)

*Goal: surface WTP indicators, competitive landscape, and referral potential. Still no pitching.*

**Q11. "When you need to solve a problem like this — a process that's too manual, too fragmented — what do you typically do first? Do you buy a tool, hire someone, build something internally, or accept the status quo?"**

> Interviewer note: Reveals their buying reflex. "Buy a tool" = software buyer. "Hire someone" = headcount buyer. "Build it" = rare in HR. "Accept the status quo" = not a buyer yet. The order of their answer matters — what comes first is their default. Grade A: "buy a tool" with a named example of something they've bought recently. Grade B: general openness to solutions. Grade C: "we make do."

**Q12. "Is anyone on your team actively looking at solutions for this right now, or has it been discussed?"**

> Interviewer note: Timing probe. If yes: "What have you looked at?" Names competitors, validates urgency. If no: "What would need to happen for it to become a priority?" This gives you the decision trigger. Grade A: active evaluation with named alternatives. Grade B: "it's on the radar." Grade C: "no, not right now."

**Q13. "Is there anyone else in your network — an HR leader, a head of mobility — who you think would have strong opinions on this kind of problem? Someone I should talk to?"**

> Interviewer note: The referral ask. Always close with this. A warm referral from a real HR buyer is the fastest way to book the next 3 calls. Don't skip it even if the call was short. Grade A: names a specific person with contact details offered. Grade B: "I can think of someone, let me check." Grade C: "no one comes to mind."

## After the call — logging and grading

### Mom Test grade rubric

| Grade | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **A** | All pain questions answered with specific past events, named consequences, quantified time/cost/frequency. Referral offered. Buying reflex = "buy a tool." |
| **B** | Mostly past-behavior answers; some generalisation. At least 1 named incident. Some emotional signal. No referral or vague referral. |
| **C** | Opinion-heavy ("I think it would be nice if…"). No specific incidents. General acknowledgement of the problem but no evidence of acute pain. Not a qualified signal. |

> **AUDIT-B10 target:** ≥5 interviews with grade ≥ B AND Buyer type = In-house buyer. A single A-grade interview with a named referral chain can bootstrap the next 2-3 calls.

### What to log immediately after the call (before the next meeting)

Using the `relopass-interview-intake` skill or manually in the Wave-5 Tracker (AUDIT-B10d):

1. **Date** — today's date
2. **Person** — full name and LinkedIn URL
3. **Company** — name, HQ country, headcount estimate
4. **Role** — exact title
5. **Buyer type** — In-house buyer / Vendor / Advisor / Other
6. **Mom Test grade** — A / B / C using the rubric above
7. **Pain score (1-5)** — 1 = aware of the problem, no urgency; 5 = actively losing sleep, recent incident
8. **WTP signal (1-5)** — 1 = "sounds useful someday"; 5 = "when can I see a demo / what does it cost"
9. **Top quote** — the single most vivid sentence they said about their pain (verbatim if possible)
10. **Persona match** — which BRAIN-3B persona (1–5) best describes them
11. **Follow-up action** — what you promised to send / who they referred / next step

### BRAIN-3B update trigger

After ≥3 calls with a Persona 4 (CHRO) or Persona 5 (Regional HRBP) match: if the evidence consistently validates or refutes their hypothesis pain/trigger, update BRAIN-3B on Notion and change their tier from ◇ to ★ (or archive them if consistently absent). This feeds BRAIN-3D (Master Company Brain).

### Persona 1 and 2 update trigger

After ≥5 calls total: if you have new verbatim quotes from real buyers, add them to the BRAIN-3B Notion page under each persona (the ★ personas currently have placeholder quotes from the 2026-05-25 build).
