## 1. The recommended positioning sentence

> **ReloPass is the EU AI Act–ready mobility platform that puts an audit trail under every AI decision your HR team relies on.**

**24 words. Plain English. No "powerful", "seamless", "journey", "all-in-one".**

What it does:
- Plants the EU AI Act flag (defensible against Topia's "agentic" narrative without trying to out-shout it).
- Names the buyer's actual fear: a regulator or auditor asking "how did your AI decide that?"
- Implies the differentiator (audit trail) without the marketing-deck shape of "we're the only…".
- The word "rely on" does emotional work — it concedes that HR is already AI-shaped and offers them a defensible version of it.

What it deliberately doesn't do:
- Doesn't use "agentic" (Topia owns that word in EU mobility). Owning the auditor narrative is sharper than fighting for a word.
- Doesn't lead with "AI" — leads with what AI does *for HR*.
- Doesn't promise to replace HR. Doesn't promise efficiency. Two of the three biggest landmines in HR-tech marketing.

## 2. Two alternatives (in case 3-buyer validation kills option 1)

### Alternative A — sharper, claim-led

> **The first mobility platform built for the EU AI Act: every decision, every document, every step — audited.**

23 words. Riskier — the "first" claim invites pushback in the validation calls. But it lands harder if the buyer is auditor-pilled. Use when the buyer says some version of "our legal team is on my back about AI."

### Alternative B — safer, description-led

> **ReloPass moves your employees across borders with the audit trail your compliance team needs and the experience yours will actually use.**

22 words. The least edgy. Uses "experience" instead of "agentic" and double-pronged on the two buyers (HR ops + employee). Good fallback if the audience is risk-averse mid-market.

## 4. The buyer-validation script (5-minute phone test)

Use this on 3 EU HR / mobility leaders from the design partner pipeline. Target: 5 minutes per call. Format: structured questions, free-form answers, single-page response.

### Setup line (30 sec)

> "I'm working on the way we describe ReloPass. I want to read you one sentence and ask three questions. Five minutes. Sound good?"
>
> Then read the recommended sentence verbatim. Pause two seconds. Then ask the three questions.

### Question 1 — gut check (90 sec)

> "If you read that sentence on a website, in your own words, what would you tell me ReloPass does?"

**What you're listening for:**
- Did they hear "audit trail" as the differentiator? (Good — message landed.)
- Did they say "AI" before "mobility"? (Mixed — they may think we're an AI company, not a mobility company.)
- Did they say "EU AI Act"? (Excellent — they registered the compliance hook.)
- Did they ignore the sentence and ask what ReloPass actually does? (Bad — sentence is too abstract.)

### Question 2 — pain match (90 sec)

> "Is the EU AI Act something that's actually on your plate right now, or is it more of a 2027 problem?"

**What you're listening for:**
- "Yes, our general counsel asked me about it last quarter." → strong signal. The positioning is timely.
- "Not yet, but I know it's coming." → workable. Position as "the platform that's ready when the deadline hits."
- "Honestly, no — we're not big enough to be in scope." → important data. May need to soften the EU AI Act framing for mid-market.
- "Yes, and we don't trust our current vendor on it." → blue-sky — sales conversation, not validation.

### Question 3 — objection surface (60 sec)

> "What would make you NOT believe that sentence?"

**What you're listening for:**
- "Show me what the audit trail actually looks like." → demo-ready objection. Healthy.
- "Anyone can say 'audit trail.'" → message risk. The proof block (004c) is what answers this.
- "I don't know what 'EU AI Act-ready' means in practice." → message risk. May need to soften jargon.
- Silence / shrug → red flag. Sentence may not be sticking.

### Wrap (30 sec)

> "Thanks. One last thing — if you could change one word in that sentence, which one?"
>
> Whatever they answer is data. Write it down verbatim.
