## Items for attestation (6)

### 1. PPS Number — Application and Uses (EU/EEA nationals) · RESIDENCE · easy-to-miss
**Claim served:** A PPS number is mandatory to start work (needed to register with Revenue) and can only be obtained once you have an address; an employer may not ask for it during recruitment. EU citizens present passport or national ID (Spanish DNI accepted) plus an Irish proof of address **dated within the last 3 months**. Newcomers often lack an Irish utility bill/bank statement, so a signed lease or third-party confirmation (landlord, hotel, hostel, school principal) is accepted to break the circle.
**Source:** citizensinformation.ie — Personal Public Service Number
**Confirm:** (a) the 3-month Irish-address requirement and the accepted alternatives are current; (b) the "employer may not request PPSN at recruitment" statement is correctly stated.

### 2. A1 Certificate — Posted Workers from Spain (EU/EEA nationals) · RESIDENCE · easy-to-miss
**Claim served:** Where a Spanish employer posts an employee to Ireland, the worker may remain in Spanish social insurance for up to 24 months under EU rules; the A1 (formerly E101) must be applied for in Spain **at least 4 weeks before** work begins in Ireland. Without it, Irish social insurance applies instead.
**Source:** citizensinformation.ie — Paying social insurance
**Confirm:** (a) the 24-month / 4-weeks-ahead specifics; (b) **applicability** — this is a *posted-worker* rule; please confirm it should be shown only when the person is posted by a Spanish employer, not to a direct local hire (ReloPass will scope accordingly).

### 3. Public Health Entitlement — Ordinary Residence (EU/EEA nationals) · RESIDENCE · easy-to-miss
**Claim served:** Entitlement to public health services depends on being *ordinarily resident* (living in Ireland ≥1 year, or provable intent to stay ≥1 year), not merely on working/paying PRSI. HSE may request proof (utility bills, bank statements, lease) not older than 12 months. Category 1 (medical card) vs Category 2 eligibility described.
**Source:** citizensinformation.ie — Entitlement to public health services
**Confirm:** the ordinary-residence test and the EU-rules medical-card route are correctly and currently stated.

### 4. PRSI — Compulsory Social Insurance (EU/EEA nationals) · RESIDENCE · easy-to-miss
**Claim served:** PRSI is compulsory for most employees over 16. **From 1 Oct 2025**, unearned income (rents, investments, dividends, interest) is liable to PRSI at **4.2%**; anyone with >€5,000 of such income is a "chargeable person" who must self-assess (under-16/over-70 exempt). **From 1 Oct 2026**, all PRSI rates rise by 0.15%.
**Source:** citizensinformation.ie — Social insurance (PRSI)
**Confirm (rate-sensitive):** the **4.2%** unearned-income rate, the **€5,000** threshold, and the **1 Oct 2025 / 1 Oct 2026** effective dates — these are the claims most likely to drift; please verify against current Revenue/DSP guidance.

### 5. Universal Social Charge — Threshold (EU/EEA nationals) · RESIDENCE · easy-to-miss
**Claim served:** USC applies once gross income exceeds **€13,000/yr**, and is then charged on the **full** income (not just the excess). Social-welfare payments exempt.
**Source:** citizensinformation.ie — Universal Social Charge
**Confirm:** the €13,000 entry threshold and the "charged on full income once crossed" mechanic are current for the applicable tax year.
